HOW THIS WORKS

One single track between the boxes. Only one train may be in it at a time, so nothing moves unless the far box says it can.

What you can see

Your own platform, loops, yard and the trains standing at them. Your neighbour's line is a lamp and nothing else. You cannot see their roads, their trains or their book.

What is in your book

Working orders and notices for your neighbour's trains and premises — never your own. Theirs has yours. That is the whole game: read a card, say it out loud, and ask for what you cannot see. Some cards are inert. Some arrive mid-shift.

Working a train

ASK LINE CLEAR — offer a train to the far box, naming where it is going.
GIVE LINE CLEAR — accept it, if you can take it. HOLD THE LINE if you cannot.
SEND INTO SECTION — once it has been given.
TO THE PLATFORM / LOOP / YARD / SHED — put an arrived train where its book says. If that road will not take it, SHUNT costs minutes and DETACH breaks it up.

A train goes where it is booked or it is shunted. It blocks the section until it is dealt with, so a road you were not told about is expensive.

The bells

One strike — somebody is asking you for the road.
Two strikes — a train is entering the section.
Two, pause, one — the section is cleared.
Telegraph chatter — a new notice has reached your book.
A wooden knock — that was refused.

How it ends

When every working is away you get a Notice of Delay: what was booked against what happened, and every card that was never read out.
